# Weeks / Pentecost

One-event draft. Shared calendar and counting evidence remain in [Feasts](../feasts.draft.md).

## Biblical names and status

Feast of Weeks (Exodus 34:22); day of firstfruits (Numbers 28:26); Pentecost (Acts 2:1; 20:16).

## Purpose and symbolism

Exodus 34:22: firstfruits of wheat harvest.

## When

Leviticus 23:15–16 counts fifty days from day after Sabbath.

## Requirements

### Food

No feast-specific command stated.

### Sacrifices/offerings

Leviticus 23:16–19; Numbers 28:26–31.

### Assembly/place/private-public

“Three times in the year shall all your males appear before the Lord GOD.” — [Exodus 23:17 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Exodus+23%3A17/)

Leviticus 23:21 requires a holy convocation.

### Fasting/self-affliction

No feast-specific command stated.

### Rest/work

Leviticus 23:21.

## Forever clause and audience

Leviticus 23:21 qualifies convocation and no ordinary work.

## Biblical celebration examples

Acts 2:1–47; Acts 20:16; 1 Corinthians 16:8 are descriptive.

Calendar/counting distinction: Leviticus 23 states the count, but does not identify a universal observation or calculation authority, location, time zone, lunar-visibility standard, month lengths, leap-month rule, or Gregorian conversion. It also does not resolve whether Leviticus 23:15 identifies weekly Sabbath or first festival rest day. Reported dates remain conditional on declared assumptions. Acts 20:16 and 1 Corinthians 16:8 are descriptive time references, not ritual proof.

### Leviticus 23:15–22

> “You shall count seven full weeks from the day after the Sabbath, from the day that you brought the sheaf of the wave offering. You shall count fifty days to the day after the seventh Sabbath. Then you shall present a grain offering of new grain to the LORD. You shall bring from your dwelling places two loaves of bread to be waved, made of two tenths of an ephah. They shall be of fine flour, and they shall be baked with leaven, as firstfruits to the LORD. And you shall present with the bread seven lambs a year old without blemish, and one bull from the herd and two rams. They shall be a burnt offering to the LORD, with their grain offering and their drink offerings, a food offering with a pleasing aroma to the LORD. And you shall offer one male goat for a sin offering, and two male lambs a year old as a sacrifice of peace offerings. And the priest shall wave them with the bread of the firstfruits as a wave offering before the LORD, with the two lambs. They shall be holy to the LORD for the priest. And you shall make proclamation on the same day. You shall hold a holy convocation. You shall not do any ordinary work. It shall be a statute forever in all your dwelling places throughout your generations. … And when you reap the harvest of your land, you shall not reap your field right up to its edge, nor shall you gather the gleanings after your harvest. You shall leave them for the poor and for the sojourner: I am the LORD your God.” — [Leviticus 23:15–22 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Leviticus+23%3A15-22/)

Do not claim prescribed offerings privately.

## What Scripture does not authorize or define

No substitute loaves, sacrifices, altar, calendar authority, place, or modern work definition.

## Conclusion

Weeks was Israel’s harvest feast with place-bound priestly offerings; Acts records Pentecost but does not command a Christian annual observance. See [Biblical Names and Status](#biblical-names-and-status), [Acts 2:1–4,37–41](#acts-2143741), and [What Scripture Does Not Authorise or Define](#what-scripture-does-not-authorize-or-define). A household or community may choose to gather, read Acts 2:4, “they were all filled with the Holy Spirit” (ESV), and pray for faithful witness. A shared harvest meal is suitable, though Scripture prescribes no food for modern Christians. These are ideas only, not biblical commands or requirements for Christians.
