## Biblical names and status

Feast of Booths (Leviticus 23:34); Feast of Tabernacles (Deuteronomy 16:16; John 7:2).

## Purpose and symbolism

> "that your generations may know that I made the people of Israel dwell in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt"

## When

Leviticus 23:34: fifteenth day of seventh month for seven days.

## Requirements

### Food

No feast-specific command stated.

### Sacrifices/offerings

Leviticus 23:35–36; Numbers 29:12–39, including “in addition to your vow offerings and your freewill offerings”.

### Assembly/place/private-public

“Three times a year all your males shall appear before the LORD your God at the place that he will choose: at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, at the Feast of Weeks, and at **the Feast of Booths**. They shall not appear before the LORD empty-handed.” — Deuteronomy 16:16 (ESV)

Leviticus 23:40,42 names fruit of splendid trees, rejoicing, and booths.

### Fasting/self-affliction

No feast-specific command stated.

### Rest/work

Leviticus 23:35.

## Forever clause and audience

Leviticus 23:41; booth command names native Israelites.

## Practical Observance Without Temple, Priest, or Jewish Community

Do not claim temple offerings or undefined public/place conditions.

### Conditional Date

["On the fifteenth day of this seventh month and for seven days"](../feasts.md#reasonable-inference).

## Conclusion

The feast remembers Israel’s dwelling in booths and retains temple offerings and native-Israelite terms; see [Purpose and Symbolism](#purpose-and-symbolism), Christians may choose a household or community outdoor gathering to read John 7:37–39, “If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink” (ESV), as a reading from the feast setting rather than a commanded fulfilment. A shared harvest meal is suitable, though Scripture prescribes no feast-specific food for modern Christians. These are ideas only, not biblical commands or requirements for Christians.
