# Feast of Dedication (Hanukkah)

Hanukkah is an eight-day Jewish festival commemorating the Temple’s purification and rededication after the Maccabean revolt. John 10:22–23 places Jesus at the Temple during this feast.

## Biblical names and status

John calls the observance “the Feast of Dedication.” The Greek of John 10:22 is *τὰ ἐγκαίνια* (*ta enkainia*), a recognised festival name meaning dedication, consecration, or rededication. “The Dedication” is more literal; English “Feast” is supplied contextually, while “Hanukkah” identifies the festival interpretively. The noun itself does not separately mean “feast.”

The [STEP SBLGNT text](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=SBLG%7Creference=John.10.22-23) reads *χειμὼν ἦν*, “it was winter,” and says Jesus was *ἐν τῇ στοᾷ τοῦ Σολομῶνος*, “in Solomon’s colonnade.”

## Purpose and symbolism

Hanukkah commemorates the purification and rededication of the Jerusalem Temple after Antiochus IV Epiphanes desecrated it and the Maccabean revolt reclaimed it. The historical account in 1 Maccabees dates the rededication to 25 Kislev, 164 BCE.

> “let us go up to purify the sanctuary and rededicate it”
>
> “For eight days they celebrated the dedication of the altar.”
>
> — Historical/deuterocanonical source: NABRE 1 Maccabees 4:36, 56. https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/1_Maccabees.4

The historical account is distinct from the later oil tradition. John’s text locates Jesus at the Temple during the feast; it does not say that he endorsed later customs. For historical overview, see https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/hanukkah/.

## When

The celebration begins on 25 Kislev and lasts eight days. NABRE 1 Maccabees 4:52 gives 25 Kislev, and NABRE 2 Maccabees 10:5–6 likewise connects the date with an eight-day celebration. https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/1_Maccabees.4 https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/2_Maccabees.10

Its season is winter in John 10:22–23. The ancient date does not yield one fixed Gregorian date without a stated Jewish calendar method and year.

## Requirements

### Food

The earliest Maccabean account describes an eight-day dedication, not a required menu. The later oil tradition is associated with Babylonian Talmud Shabbat 21b. https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.21b?lang=bi

This later tradition should not be projected into John 10:22–23 or treated as Jesus’ endorsement.

### Sacrifices/offerings

The rededication account concerns the Temple and altar. It does not establish a sacrifice or offering requirement for later observers in the sources cited here.

### Assembly/place/private-public

John’s setting is public: Jerusalem’s Temple, in Solomon’s colonnade. The text reports Jesus’ presence there; it does not prescribe a place or assembly rule for later observance.

### Fasting/self-affliction

No fasting or self-affliction requirement is stated in the cited accounts.

### Rest/work

No Torah-commanded day of rest is stated for Hanukkah. The feast table therefore lists no Torah-commanded rest.

## Forever clause and audience

The Maccabean event is later than the Hebrew Bible’s historical and prophetic corpus. That chronology does not establish a simple claim about when any canon “closed.”

1–2 Maccabees are outside the Jewish Tanakh and typical Protestant Old Testament, but Catholic Bibles receive them as deuterocanonical. Orthodox collections vary more widely. https://www.sefaria.org/texts/Tanakh https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_1 https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2H.HTM https://orthodoxwiki.org/Biblical_canon

John’s account identifies a Jewish festival setting. It does not add a Torah command, a forever clause, or a required Christian observance.

## Biblical celebration examples

The Maccabean account records the rededication and its eight-day celebration. John 10:22–23 later describes Jesus in Jerusalem during the Feast of Dedication. These are historical descriptions, not a command to observe the festival.

## Every feast-specific scripture

> “At that time the Feast of Dedication took place at Jerusalem. It was winter, and Jesus was walking in the temple, in the colonnade of Solomon.” — John 10:22–23 (ESV)

This preserves the text’s claim: the Feast of Dedication was at Jerusalem, it was winter, and Jesus was in Solomon’s colonnade. The passage locates him during the feast; it does not endorse later customs or institute an annual rite.

## Practical Observance Without Temple, Priest, or Jewish Community

No rite is prescribed for Christians or other later communities in John 10:22–23. Historical study and voluntary remembrance should not be presented as a Torah requirement or as Jesus’ endorsement of later Hanukkah customs.

### Conditional Date

Date formula: $B_{y,9}+24$, using Kislev as month 9 under the stated calendar convention. Hanukkah runs for eight days from that date. A Gregorian conversion remains conditional on the calendar method and year.

## What Scripture does not authorize or define

John 10:22–23 does not institute Hanukkah as a Torah-appointed feast, establish an annual command, prescribe food, command rest, or define later oil customs. The later Maccabean historical accounts explain the feast’s origin without changing what John’s text says.

## Conclusion

Hanukkah remembers the [Maccabean rededication](#purpose-and-symbolism) on 25 Kislev and its eight-day celebration. [John’s account](#every-feast-specific-scripture) places Jesus at the Temple during the feast without making later customs or annual observance a command.
