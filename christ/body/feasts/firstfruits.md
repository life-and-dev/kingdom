# Wave-Sheaf / Firstfruits / Yom HaBikkurim (יוֹם הַבִּכּוּרִים)

Also known as "Feast of Harvest".

> None shall appear before me empty-handed. You shall keep the Feast of Harvest, of the firstfruits of your labor, of what you sow in the field. -- Exodus 23:15-16 (ESV)

> And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, “Speak to the people of Israel and say to them,
>
> When you come into the land that I give you and reap its harvest, you shall bring the sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest to the priest, and he shall wave the sheaf before the LORD, so that you may be accepted. On the day after the Sabbath the priest shall wave it.
>
> And on the day when you wave the sheaf, you shall offer a male lamb a year old without blemish as a burnt offering to the Lord.
>
> And the grain offering with it shall be two tenths of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil, a food offering to the LORD with a pleasing aroma, band the drink offering with it shall be of wine, a fourth of a hin.
>
> And you shall eat neither bread nor grain parched or fresh until this same day, until you have brought the offering of your God: it is a statute forever throughout your generations in all your dwellings.
>
> -- Leviticus 23:9-14 (ESV)

## Biblical names and status

Leviticus 23:10–11 names “the sheaf of the firstfruits” and “the wave offering”; it is a priestly rite, not separately named annual feast.

## Purpose and symbolism

“Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, When you come into the land that I give you and reap its harvest, you shall bring the sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest to the priest, …” — Leviticus 23:10 (ESV)

## When

“… so that you may be accepted. On the day after the Sabbath the priest shall wave it.” — Leviticus 23:11 (ESV)

“You shall count seven full weeks from the day after the Sabbath, from the day that you brought the sheaf of the wave offering. You shall count fifty days to the day after the seventh Sabbath. Then you shall present a grain offering of new grain to the LORD.” — [Leviticus 23:15–16 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Leviticus+23%3A15-16/)

This is counting evidence for Weeks; it does not resolve which Sabbath fixes a date.

## Requirements

### Food

Leviticus 23:14 prohibits bread, parched grain, and fresh grain before offering.

### Sacrifices/offerings

Leviticus 23:12–13: male lamb without blemish, grain offering, and drink offering.

The priests and their families enjoyed the offerings of Firstfruit (Numbers 18:8-20).

### Assembly/place/private-public

Leviticus 23:10 requires land, harvest, and priest.

### Fasting/self-affliction

No feast-specific command stated.

### Rest/work

No feast-specific command stated.

## Forever clause and audience

Leviticus 23:14 qualifies grain prohibition until offering.

## Biblical celebration examples

Joshua 5:10–12 is probable, not reported wave rite. 1 Corinthians 15:20–23 is interpretive/allusive, not observance.

## Every feast-specific scripture

> “While the people of Israel were encamped at Gilgal, they kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the month in the evening on the plains of Jericho. And the day after the Passover, on that very day, they ate of the produce of the land, unleavened cakes and parched grain. And the manna ceased the day after they ate of the produce of the land. And there was no longer manna for the people of Israel, but they ate of the fruit of the land of Canaan that year.” — Joshua 5:10–12 (ESV)

> “But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep. For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive. But each in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, then at his coming those who belong to Christ.” — 1 Corinthians 15:20–23 (ESV)

> “And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying, ‘Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, When you come into the land that I give you and reap its harvest, you shall bring the sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest to the priest, and he shall wave the sheaf before the LORD, so that you may be accepted. On the day after the Sabbath the priest shall wave it. And on the day when you wave the sheaf, you shall offer a male lamb a year old without blemish as a burnt offering to the LORD. And the grain offering with it shall be two tenths of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil, a food offering to the LORD with a pleasing aroma, and the drink offering with it shall be of wine, a fourth of a hin. And you shall eat neither bread nor grain parched or fresh until this same day, until you have brought the offering of your God: it is a statute forever throughout your generations in all your dwellings.’” — Leviticus 23:9–14 (ESV)

Joshua does not name a sheaf, a priest, or waving. It is probable only, not an explicitly recorded priestly wave-sheaf performance. Firstfruits language in 1 Corinthians 15 is interpretive/allusive, not observance instruction.

## Prosperity Promise

> "Honor the Lord with your wealth, with the firstfruits of all your crops; then your barns will be filled to overflowing, and your vats will brim over with new wine." -- Proverbs 3:9-10 (ESV)

This is a general principle and not a festival commandment or guarenteed condition.

## Conclusion

The wave sheaf was a land-and-harvest priestly rite, not a separately named Christian feast; see [Biblical Names and Status](#biblical-names-and-status), [Sacrifices/Offerings](#sacrificesofferings). Christians may choose a household or community thanksgiving gathering and read 1 Corinthians 15:20, “Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits” (ESV), without waving an object or offering a sacrifice. Seasonal produce may be shared, but Scripture prescribes no food for a modern Christian observance. These are ideas only, not biblical commands or requirements for Christians.
