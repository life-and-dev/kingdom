# Trumpet Memorial

> “And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying, ‘Speak to the people of Israel, saying, In the seventh month, on the first day of the month, you shall observe a **day of solemn rest**, a memorial proclaimed with **blast of trumpets, a holy convocation**. You shall not do any ordinary work, and you shall **present a food offering** to the LORD.’” — Leviticus 23:23–25 (ESV)

This is general priestly-trumpet evidence, not a named historical Trumpet Memorial celebration.

> “On the first day of the seventh month you shall have a **holy convocation**.
>
> You shall **not do any ordinary work**.
>
> It is a day for you to **blow the trumpets**, and you shall offer a burnt offering, for a pleasing aroma to the LORD: one bull from the herd, one ram, seven male lambs a year old without blemish; also their grain offering of fine flour mixed with oil, three tenths of an ephah for the bull, two tenths for the ram, and one tenth for each of the seven lambs; with one male goat for a **sin offering, to make atonement** for you; besides the burnt offering of the new moon, and its grain offering, and the regular burnt offering and its grain offering, and their drink offering, according to the rule for them, for a pleasing aroma, a food offering to the LORD.
>
> — Numbers 29:1-6 (ESV)

## Biblical names and status

Leviticus 23:24: seventh-month appointed day.

## Purpose and symbolism

> “… a memorial proclaimed with blast of trumpets, a holy convocation.” — Leviticus 23:24 (ESV)

## When

Leviticus 23:24; Numbers 29:1: first day of seventh month.

## Requirements

### Food

No feast-specific command stated.

### Sacrifices/offerings

Numbers 29:2-6 prescribes "a sin offering, to make atonement for you.”

The Mosaic procedure also requires offerings at the place the LORD chooses: “you shall offer your burnt offerings” there (Deuteronomy 12:13-14). Leviticus 17:4-6 requires bringing an offering to entrance of tent of meeting for presentation to the LORD and priestly handling at the altar. Aaron and his sons are appointed to priesthood (Exodus 28:1), and Numbers 3:10 states that an unauthorized person who comes near must be put to death. In this context, “unauthorized person” concerns non-Aaronic approach to priestly service, not ethnicity.

Hebrews 10:8-18 explicitly presents Christ’s once-for-all offering and says, “there is no longer any offering for sin.”

### Assembly/place/private-public

Leviticus 23:24–25.

### Fasting/self-affliction

No feast-specific command stated.

### Rest/work

> “You shall not do any ordinary work.” — Numbers 29:1 (ESV)

## Forever clause and audience

No feast-specific forever clause stated. Nor does the temple or priestly service exist to accommodate the sin atonement.

It is possible that this Psalm could also refer to this holy day:

> **Sing aloud** to God our strength;  
> Shout for **joy** to the God of Jacob!  
> **Raise a song**: Sound the tambourine, the sweet lyre with the harp.  
> **Blow the trumpet** at the new moon, at the full moon, on **our feast day**.
> For it is a **statute for Israel**, a rule of the God of Jacob.
>
> — Psalm 81:1-4 (ESV)

Numbers 10:10 may provides broader ritual background for the Feast of Trumpets: priestly trumpet-blowing accompanied Israel’s appointed feasts, new moons, and sacrifices. Yet Leviticus 23:24 and Numbers 29:1 do not name the instrument or explicitly cite Numbers 10. The connection is therefore probable liturgical context, not an explicit identification of that feast with the silver trumpets.

> “On the day of your gladness also, and at your appointed feasts and at the beginnings of your months, you shall [blow the trumpets](trumpets.md) over your burnt offerings and over the sacrifices of your peace offerings. They shall be a reminder of you before your God: I am the LORD your God.” — Numbers 10:10 (ESV)

## Conclusion

Trumpet Memorial was an Israelite appointed day with priestly offerings that must not be recreated; see [Sacrifices/Offerings](#sacrificesofferings) and [Conditional Date](#conditional-date). A household or community may choose to gather, read 1 Thessalonians 4:16, “the sound of the trumpet of God” (ESV), and pray in hope of the Lord’s return without identifying that text as fulfilment of this feast. A shared meal is suitable, though Scripture prescribes no food for a modern Christian observance. These are ideas only, not biblical commands or requirements for Christians.
