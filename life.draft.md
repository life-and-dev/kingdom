# Life in the Kingdom of God

*What is life in the Kingdom of God?*

1. Some would limit the Kingdom of God to their church denomination.
2. Others would say it only starts in the afterlife when we meet God in Heaven.

> [!WARNING]
> Greek philosophy and Christian teaching have offered **different accounts of the soul and human destiny**, yet many Christians still believe that the *[immortal soul](death/immortal.md)* of a Christian goes *[immediately and conciously to heaven to live with God](https://church.ofgod.info/myths/heaven)*. While it is unclear where this unbiblical idea originates, the striking parallel with Greek philosophy and Roman mythology may have [influenced this Christian belief](https://church.ofgod.info/evolution) in this regard.

## The Kingdom of Heaven

The Kingdom of Heaven is [God's everlasting rule](index.md#when-is-the-kingdom-of-god), not a distant place or a physical territory (Psalm 145:13). The God the Father rules from heaven, but His authority extends to earth. Jesus taught his disciples to pray:

> Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth **as it is in [heaven](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/heaven)**. — Matthew 6:10 (ESV)

God the Father has given Jesus authority to rule under Him (Matthew 28:18; 1 Corinthians 15:28). Jesus announced that the Kingdom had drawn near, called people to repent and believe the good news, and demonstrated God's concern through healing and deliverance (Mark 1:14-15; Matthew 4:23).

Paul told believers that the Father had delivered them from darkness and transferred them into the Kingdom of His beloved Son (Colossians 1:12-14). Through Jesus, God [restores what Adam lost](index.md#what-jesus-restored), including His presence, peace and eternal life.

Those who [humbly trust and obey](index.md#who-is-part-of-the-kingdom-of-god) the Father and His Son are called to seek God's Kingdom above status and possessions, and to serve one another (Matthew 6:24,33; 18:1-4; 20:25-28). The Kingdom is marked by [righteousness, peace and joy](index.md#what-is-the-kingdom-of-god) (Romans 14:17).

The Kingdom also has a future fulfilment: Jesus taught his followers to pray for its coming, and Paul looked forward to the destruction of death (Matthew 6:10; 1 Corinthians 15:24-26).

## Death

Genesis places human life on earth under God's authority. God blessed the first humans and told them to be fruitful, fill the earth, and exercise dominion over living creatures (Genesis 1:28). He placed the man in Eden to work and keep the garden (Genesis 2:15). Eden can be understood as an early picture of human life under God's rule.

Adam's disobedience brought death to mankind (Romans 5:12-19). The obedience of Jesus and brought the gift of [righteousness](righteousness.md) and life.

[Scripture describes death as the loss of life](death.md), not as life continuing unchanged elsewhere. Ecclesiastes 9:5 says that **the dead know nothing**, and Psalm 146:4 describes **human plans perishing** when a person returns to the earth. Daniel 12:2 compares the dead to those **asleep** in the dust who will awake.

### Salvation

Salvation is God's deliverance of people from sin and [permanent eternal destruction](death.md). Christians still die, but God promises [resurrection](death/resurrection.md) and eternal life through Jesus Christ (Daniel 12:2; John 3:16-17). God the Father sent Jesus, whose death and resurrection stand at the centre of that deliverance (Romans 4:25). Eternal life is God's gift through Jesus Christ, not a wage people earn (Romans 6:23).

Jesus called people to **repent and believe** the good news (Mark 1:14-15). To believe the good news is to have [faith](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith): to trust God and the Son he sent, not merely to acknowledge that they exist.

- **Repentance** means turning from sin to obeying God
- **Faith** means trusting God and the Son he sent.

**Baptism and a changed life** are fitting responses to the good news. These responses do not purchase forgiveness. They express reliance on God's mercy. The apostles called hearers to repent and be baptised in Jesus Christ's name (Acts 2:38), and Paul proclaimed the risen Jesus and called for faith (Romans 10:9-10).

Repentance also has practical consequences. John the Baptist called for **fruit consistent with repentance** and urged those with food and clothing to share (Luke 3:8-11). Jesus taught his disciples to love one another (John 13:34-35). Such actions express a changed direction.

A changed life, is a **life build on trust** (faith) which involves more than acknowledging that a message exists. Abraham trusted God's ability to fulfil His promise (Romans 4:20-21). Likewise, the call to believe the good news concerns reliance on God and the one he sent. The local study of [faith](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith.md) examines this trust.

### Righteousness

[Righteousness](life/righteousness.md) concerns being **right before God and living in a way that reflects his character**. Abraham trusted God's promise, and his [faith](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith.md) was counted to him as righteousness (Genesis 15:6; Romans 4:20-25). Paul connects justification by faith with peace with God through Jesus Christ (Romans 5:1).

The Father desires a loving relationship with his people, not mere outward compliance. He showed His love by sending His son so that people might have life through him (1 John 4:9-10). Those who receive Christ and believe in his name are given the right to become **children of God** (John 1:12). As a father shows compassion to his children, so the LORD shows compassion to those who fear him (Psalm 103:13).

> For you did not receive **the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear**, but you have received **the Spirit of adoption as sons**, by whom we cry, “Abba! Father!” The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that **we are children of God**, and if children, then heirs—heirs of God and fellow heirs with Christ, provided we suffer with him in order that we may also be glorified with him.
>
> — Romans 8:15-17 (ESV)

Believers can approach God as their Father rather than live in fear as though they were only slaves. Their **obedience is a response to His love** and belongs to their life as His children (Romans 8:15). As John writes, “We love because he first loved us” (1 John 4:19). Jesus teaches that **the greatest commandments are to love** God and to love one's neighbour (Matthew 22:37-40).

**Righteousness is not optional.** A single confession, considered apart from continuing trust and obedience, is not a substitute for a life turned towards God.

> Every tree that does **not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire**. Thus you will recognize them by their fruits. Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but **the one who does the will of my Father** who is in heaven. On that day many will say to me, ‘Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and do many mighty works in your name?’ And then will I declare to them, ‘I never knew you; depart from me, you workers of **lawlessness**.’
>
> “Everyone then who hears these words of mine and **does them** will be like a **wise** man who built his house on the rock.
>
> — Matthew 7:19-24 (ESV)

Jesus warned that confession is not enough for salvation. Doing "the will of the Father" is a lifestyle of obedience. James describes faith without works as dead (James 2:14-17). These passages make righteous living important without presenting good deeds as a bribe that purchases salvation.

God saves by grace through faith, and good works are the intended fruit of that grace (Ephesians 2:8-10). Believers who sin are called to confess rather than pretend to be without sin (1 John 1:8-9). Righteous living is not a claim that a person has never needed forgiveness.

The prophets also connect righteousness with how people treat others. The LORD calls for justice and care for the vulnerable, rather than religious observance that ignores wrongdoing (Isaiah 1:16-17). Jesus likewise identifies justice, mercy, and faithfulness as weighty matters (Matthew 23:23). Such conduct reflects the character of the God whom believers trust.

## Afterlife

Through Jesus, God the Father brings people into His son's kingdom (Colossians 1:13-14). The good news concerns forgiveness and reconciliation, not a return to paradise achieved by human effort.

This restoration has begun, but its completion remains future. Paul says that death is the last enemy to be destroyed (1 Corinthians 15:25-26). The biblical hope thus moves from creation and human failure towards redemption, resurrection, and a renewed creation.

### Resurrection

The biblical hope is that [God will raise the dead](death/resurrection.md). Daniel describes those sleeping in the dust awakening, some to everlasting life and some to shame (Daniel 12:2). Jesus likewise speaks of people coming out of their tombs to life or judgment (John 5:28-29).

**God the Father raised Jesus from the dead.** Paul calls Jesus the firstfruits of those who have died. His resurrection is the foundation of the hope that those who belong to him **will also be made alive** (1 Corinthians 15:20-23).

Paul places their resurrection at Christ's coming and at the last trumpet. The dead will be raised imperishable, and believers who are still alive will be changed (1 Corinthians 15:22-23,51-54; 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17).

To explain the resurrection body, Paul compares what is buried to a seed and says that God gives it a body as He chooses. What is sown perishable is raised imperishable; what is sown in weakness is raised in power (1 Corinthians 15:35-44).

**Resurrection gives hope to those who have died** without requiring them to possess an [indestructible soul](death/immortal.md).

### Judgment

God's judgment takes human conduct seriously. Ecclesiastes 12:13-14 says that **God will bring every deed into judgment**, including what is hidden. Paul says that each person will give an account to God (Romans 14:10-12), and Acts 17:31 says God has appointed Jesus to judge the world in righteousness.

Jesus' account of the sheep and the goats connects judgment with how people treated those in need (Matthew 25:31-46). Judgment according to deeds does not mean that human effort purchases salvation. **All need God's mercy** and the deliverance he provides through Jesus (Romans 3:23-24). The deeds considered in judgment show that trust and its absence have consequences in life.

Scripture presents both **eternal life and [destruction](death/judgement.md) as real outcomes**, rather than assuming that everyone receives the same inheritance (John 5:28-29). Jesus speaks of those in the tombs coming out, so judgment is connected with the raising of the dead, not merely with events during the present life.

The promise of righteous judgment also means that wrongdoing is not ignored. The judge appointed by God will judge the world in righteousness, and God has given assurance by raising him from the dead (Acts 17:31). This warning belongs alongside the invitation to repent and receive God's mercy. See [God's Judgement](death/judgement.md).

### New Earth

God remembers **those who fear Him** and promises to spare them as His treasured possession (Malachi 3:16-18).

God promises new heavens and a new earth that will remain before Him (Isaiah 66:22-23). His children will **live in His presence** and rejoice in His salvation. He will swallow up death and wipe away tears (Isaiah 25:8-9). There will be **no more death, mourning, crying, or pain** (Revelation 21:3-4). Instead, God's people will enjoy fellowship with Him, whose presence brings fullness of joy (Psalm 16:11).

Isaiah describes people building homes, enjoying their harvests, and living in peace: God hears them, and the wolf and lamb feed together (Isaiah 65:21-25). Unlike Plato's account of virtuous souls returning to their stars, this picture places life on earth. Isaiah 65:20 still mentions death, however, while Revelation 21:4 says death will be no more; the descriptions should not be treated as identical without acknowledging that difference.

Isaiah 66:22-24 places enduring worship in the new creation alongside a view of the corpses of those who rebelled against the LORD. The passage includes judgment as well as the worship of those who remain; it does not say that the corpses are living worshippers.

Revelation describes the holy city as the bride, the Lamb's wife, using images of gates, foundations, measurements, and precious materials (Revelation 21:9-21). Its splendour need not require every feature to be literal. The vision continues with God dwelling with His people, the nations walking in the city's light, and the tree's leaves for their healing (Revelation 21:3,24; 22:1-2).

Paul's promise of resurrection explains the hope of **life beyond death**, while creation itself is to be freed from corruption (1 Corinthians 15:20-26; Romans 8:19-23).

#### The Earth as an Inheritance

Isaiah describes a feast and the removal of death and disgrace:

> And in this mountain shall the LORD of hosts make unto all people a feast of fat things, a feast of wines on the lees, of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined. And he will destroy in this mountain the face of the covering cast over all people, and the vail that is spread over all nations. He will **swallow up death in victory**; and the Lord GOD will **wipe away tears** from off all faces; and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the LORD hath spoken it. And it shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the LORD; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation. — Isaiah 25:6-9 (KJV)

The feast is for all peoples, and salvation comes from the LORD. Paul cites the promise that death will be swallowed up when he discusses resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:54).

#### Isaiah's New Creation

Isaiah speaks of new heavens and a new earth in language of joy, safety, work, and peace:

> For, behold, I create **new heavens and a new earth**: and the former shall not be remembered, nor come into mind. But be ye glad and rejoice for ever in that which I create: for, behold, I create Jerusalem a rejoicing, and her people a joy. And I will rejoice in Jerusalem, and joy in my people: and the voice of weeping shall be no more heard in her, nor the voice of crying. — Isaiah 65:17-19 (KJV)

> There shall be no more thence an infant of days, nor an old man that hath not filled his days: for the child shall **die** an hundred years old; but the sinner being an hundred years old shall be accursed. And they shall build houses, and inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, and eat the fruit of them. They shall not build, and another inhabit; they shall not plant, and another eat: for as the days of a tree are the days of my people, and mine elect shall long enjoy the work of their hands. They shall not labour in vain, nor bring forth for trouble; for they are the seed of the blessed of the LORD, and their offspring with them. — Isaiah 65:20-23 (KJV)

> And it shall come to pass, that before they call, I will answer; and while they are yet speaking, I will hear. The wolf and the lamb shall feed together, and the lion shall eat straw like the bullock: and dust shall be the serpent's meat. They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain, saith the LORD. — Isaiah 65:24-25 (KJV)

The passage depicts secure homes, fruitful work, answered prayer, and peace. Yet Isaiah 65:20 still speaks of death, whereas Revelation 21:4 says death will be no more. The passages differ in their imagery and descriptions. *They may describe different stages or use different prophetic perspectives*, but neither explanation should erase the tension or be presented as an explicit statement of the texts.

Isaiah closes with a promise that the new creation and God's people will endure, and that all flesh will worship:

> For as the **new heavens and the new earth**, which I will make, shall remain before me, saith the LORD, so shall your seed and your name remain. And it shall come to pass, that from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me, saith the LORD. And they shall go forth, and look upon the **carcases** of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring unto all flesh. — Isaiah 66:22-24 (KJV)

The closing image is disturbing: worshippers see the corpses of those who rebelled against the LORD. It places judgment beside the promise of enduring worship, rather than describing the corpses as living worshippers.

#### Creation Set Free

Paul connects the future of God's children with the future of creation itself:

> For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the **glory** which shall be revealed in us. For the earnest expectation of the creature waiteth for the manifestation of the sons of God. For the creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who hath subjected the same in hope, Because the creature itself also shall be **delivered from the bondage of corruption** into the glorious liberty of the children of God. — Romans 8:18-21 (KJV)

> For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now. And not only they, but ourselves also, which have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption, to wit, the **redemption of our body**. For we are saved by hope: but hope that is seen is not hope: for what a man seeth, why doth he yet hope for? But if we hope for that we see not, then do we with patience wait for it. — Romans 8:22-25 (KJV)

In this KJV passage, “creature” refers to creation. Paul anticipates both creation's release from corruption and the redemption of believers' bodies. His hope includes the world God made, not merely departure from it.

Peter also looks towards God's promise, while warning of judgment:

> But the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night; in the which the **heavens shall pass away** with a great noise, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, the **earth also and the works** that are therein shall be burned up. Seeing then that all these things shall be dissolved, what manner of persons ought ye to be in all holy conversation and godliness, Looking for and hasting unto the coming of the day of God, wherein the heavens being on fire shall be dissolved, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat? Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for **new heavens and a new earth**, wherein dwelleth righteousness. — 2 Peter 3:10-13 (KJV)

Peter's language of fire and dissolution warns against treating the present order as permanent. The promise is a new creation characterised by righteousness. The passage does not require every detail of how God brings it about to be resolved here.

#### God Dwells With His People

Revelation confirms the hope of new creation in a vision of the holy city coming down from God:

> And I saw a **new heaven and a new earth**: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea. And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, **coming down from God out of heaven**, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the **tabernacle of God is with men**, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God. — Revelation 21:1-3 (KJV)

> And God shall **wipe away all tears** from their eyes; and there shall be **no more death**, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away. And he that sat upon the throne said, Behold, I make all things new. And he said unto me, Write: for these words are true and faithful. — Revelation 21:4-5 (KJV)

> And he said unto me, It is done. I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end. I will give unto him that is athirst of the fountain of the water of life freely. He that overcometh shall inherit all things; and I will be his God, and he shall be my son. But the fearful, and unbelieving, and the abominable, and murderers, and sorcerers, and idolaters, and all liars, shall have their part in the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death. — Revelation 21:6-8 (KJV)

The vision joins God's presence and freely given life with a warning of judgment. Its city comes down from heaven; its central promise is that God dwells with his people.

The city is presented through symbolic imagery as the bride, the Lamb's wife:

> And there came unto me one of the seven angels which had the seven vials full of the seven last plagues, and talked with me, saying, Come hither, I will shew thee the **bride, the Lamb's wife**. And he carried me away in the spirit to a great and high mountain, and shewed me that great city, the holy Jerusalem, descending out of heaven from God, Having the glory of God: and her light was like unto a stone most precious, even like a jasper stone, clear as crystal; And had a wall great and high, and had twelve gates, and at the gates twelve angels, and names written thereon, which are the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel: On the east three gates; on the north three gates; on the south three gates; and on the west three gates. And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and in them the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb. — Revelation 21:9-14 (KJV)

> And he that talked with me had a golden reed to measure the city, and the gates thereof, and the wall thereof. And the city lieth foursquare, and the length is as large as the breadth: and he measured the city with the reed, twelve thousand furlongs. The length and the breadth and the height of it are equal. And he measured the wall thereof, an hundred and forty and four cubits, according to the measure of a man, that is, of the angel. And the building of the wall of it was of jasper: and the city was pure gold, like unto clear glass. — Revelation 21:15-18 (KJV)

> And the foundations of the wall of the city were garnished with all manner of precious stones. The first foundation was jasper; the second, sapphire; the third, a chalcedony; the fourth, an emerald; The fifth, sardonyx; the sixth, sardius; the seventh, chrysolyte; the eighth, beryl; the ninth, a topaz; the tenth, a chrysoprasus; the eleventh, a jacinth; the twelfth, an amethyst. And the twelve gates were twelve pearls: every several gate was of one pearl: and the street of the city was pure gold, as it were transparent glass. — Revelation 21:19-21 (KJV)

John continues with images of light, the nations, and access to the city:

> And I saw no temple therein: for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it. And the city had no need of the sun, neither of the moon, to shine in it: for the **glory of God** did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof. And the nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it: and the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honour into it. And the gates of it shall not be shut at all by day: for there shall be no night there. And they shall bring the glory and honour of the nations into it. And there shall in no wise enter into it any thing that defileth, neither whatsoever worketh abomination, or maketh a lie: but they which are written in the Lamb's book of life. — Revelation 21:22-27 (KJV)

> And he shewed me a pure **river of water of life**, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb. In the midst of the street of it, and on either side of the river, was there the **tree of life**, which bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit every month: and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations. And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him: And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads. And there shall be no night there; and they need no candle, neither light of the sun; for the Lord God giveth them light: and they shall reign for ever and ever. — Revelation 22:1-5 (KJV)

The river, tree, light, and city are elements of a prophetic vision. They communicate life, healing, service, and God's presence without requiring every image to be read as a literal construction detail. Unlike Plato's account of souls returning to stars, this vision centres on God's victory over death and his renewed creation.

### Glory

God's [glory](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/glory) is His revealed greatness, worth, and splendour. People **glorify Him by recognising His character** and responding with praise, trust, and faithful living. The heavens declare God's glory (Psalm 19:1), and His people are called to declare His glory among the nations (Psalm 96:3).

Abraham gave glory to God by trusting that God could fulfil his promise (Romans 4:20-21). Faith is not confidence in one's own *[strength of belief](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/terms/faith)*. **Faith is reliance on God and His promise.**

Jesus taught that **good works can lead others to glorify God the Father** (Matthew 5:16). Such works are not a way to earn salvation. They express a life shaped by trust in the God who saves. The future glory promised to God's children also belongs to his purpose of restoring creation (Romans 8:17-21).

Paul compares present suffering with the future glory God will reveal to his children. He also says creation awaits freedom from corruption **when they share in that glory** (Romans 8:18-21). This hope points to God's power and generosity, rather than to honour people earn for themselves. In the meantime, trust and faithful conduct honour him.

The psalmist calls on the nations to acknowledge the LORD's glory and strength (Psalm 96:7-9). This response includes **worship and recognition of His rule**, not merely admiration for the world He made. God's people can direct others' attention to Him through both praise and conduct.

## Conclusion

The biblical hope rests on God's gift: his [kingdom](#the-kingdom-of-heaven) reaches earth, and his [salvation](#salvation) calls for trust and a transformed life. Believers look to [resurrection](#resurrection), [judgment](#judgment), and [eternal life](#eternal-life) in God's promised [new earth](#new-earth), where his people honour him and dwell in his presence.
