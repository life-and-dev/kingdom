# Salvation

There are no scripture that state do this thing and you are saved forever. There are also no fixed list of steps to follow for salvation. Therefore, I have listed biblical examples that shows how people were saved. The purpose is to establish a pattern for salvation, rather than to read doctrines back into scripture.

## Salvation Before Christ

### Moses' Teaching

Moses taught Israel to love the Father and obey his commands:

> Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall **love the LORD your God** with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might. — Deuteronomy 6:4-5 (ESV)

Hebrew אָהַב (’ahav) means love, including affection. Here it calls for wholehearted inward devotion expressed in loyalty, service and obedience (Deuteronomy 11:1), not forced romantic feelings or mere outward compliance.

> And now, Israel, what does the LORD your God require of you, but
>
> - to fear the LORD your God,
> - to walk in all His ways,
> - to love Him,
> - to serve the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul, and
> - to keep the commandments and statutes of the LORD,
>
> which I am commanding you today for your good? — Deuteronomy 10:12-13

In the book of Leviticus it is also written:

> You shall therefore **keep My statutes and My rules**; if a person does them, he shall **live** by them: I am the LORD. — Leviticus 18:5 (ESV)

Moses also spoke of Israel returning after being scattered, and of the Father restoring its fortunes:

> and **return to the LORD your God**, you and your children, and **obey His voice** in all that I command you today, with all your heart and with all your soul, then the LORD your God will **restore your fortunes** and have mercy on you, and he will gather you again from all the peoples where the LORD your God has scattered you. — Deuteronomy 30:2-3 (ESV)

Isaiah calles for a restored relationship with the LORD by using intimite terms like "seek", "call". This is only possible if the wicked respond by forsaking his sinful way and to return to the the LORD:

> “**Seek the LORD** while He may be found;  
> **call upon Him** while He is near;  
> let the wicked **forsake his way**, and the unrighteous man his thoughts;
> let him **return to the LORD**, that He may have compassion on him,
> and to our God for He will abundantly **pardon**.
>
> — Isaiah 55:6-7 (ESV)

### Paul's Interpretation

Paul cites Abraham and David when discussing [faith](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith), [righteousness](righteousness.md) and [forgiveness](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/forgiveness) apart from works (Romans 4:1-8). He also connects the Father's passing over former sins with Jesus' death:

> This was to show God’s righteousness, because in His divine forbearance He had **passed over former sins**. — Romans 3:25 (ESV)

Paul's explanation does not establish that Abraham, David or other earlier believers knew the later details of Jesus' death and resurrection.

## Laws Required For Salvation

> But some men came down from Judea and were teaching the brothers, **“Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved.”** And after Paul and Barnabas had no small dissension and debate with them, Paul and Barnabas and some of the others were appointed to go up to Jerusalem to the apostles and the elders about this question. So, being sent on their way by the church, they passed through both Phoenicia and Samaria, describing in detail the conversion of the Gentiles, and brought great joy to all the brothers. >
> When they came to Jerusalem, they were welcomed by the church and the apostles and the elders, and they declared all that God had done with them. But some believers who belonged to the party of the Pharisees rose up and said, **“It is necessary to circumcise them and to order them to keep the law of Moses.”**
>
> The apostles and the elders were gathered together to consider this matter. And after there had been much debate, Peter stood up and said to them, “Brothers, you know that in the early days God made a choice among you, that by my mouth the Gentiles should hear the word of the gospel and believe. And **God, Who knows the heart, bore witness to them, by giving them the Holy Spirit just as he did to us, and he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by [faith](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith).** Now, therefore, why are you putting God to the test by placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear? But we believe that **we will be saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus**, just as they will.”
>
> And all the assembly fell silent, and they listened to Barnabas and Paul as they related what signs and wonders God had done through them among the Gentiles. — Acts 15:1-12 (ESV)

After Paul's testimony James quoted the prophets to show that it had always been part of God's plan to reach the non-Jewish nations. (Acts 15:13-18)

> Therefore my judgment is that **we should not trouble those of the Gentiles who turn to God**, but should write to them to
>
> 1. abstain from the things polluted by idols, and
> 2. *[abstain]* from sexual immorality, and
> 3. *[abstain]* from what has been strangled, and
> 4. *[abstain]* from blood.
>
> For from ancient generations Moses has had in every city those who proclaim him, for he is read every Sabbath in the synagogues.” — Acts 15:19-21 (ESV)

The apostles then choose Barsabbas and Silas to send with Paul with a letter that confirms the decision:

> Since we have heard that some persons have gone out from us and troubled you with words, unsettling your minds, although we gave them no instructions, it has seemed good to us, having come to one accord, to choose men and send them to you...
>
> For it has **seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to lay on you no greater burden than these requirements** that you:
>
> 1. abstain from what has been sacrificed to idols *[Exodus 34:15; Leviticus 17:1-9]*, and
> 2. *[abstain]* from blood *[Leviticus 17:10-16; Deuteronomy 12:23-24]*, and
> 3. *[abstain]* from what has been strangled, and
> 4. *[abstain]* from sexual immorality *[Leviticus 18:6-23,26]*.
>
> If you keep yourselves from these, **you will do well**. Farewell.”
>
> — Acts 15:24-25,28-29

> [!TIP]
> Although the Torah never explicitly forbid strangling animals, some place law #3 (strangled) and law #4 (blood) together because a strangled animal still contain its blood.

Many modern Christians quote Acts 15:28-29 out of context to show that *only these 4 laws should be obeyed*. This is often done in combination of misquoting Hebrews 8:13 out of context to show that the "Old Testament" is now *obsolute*. Yet, Jesus himself said:

> "**Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law** or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them. For truly, I say to you, **until heaven and earth pass away, not an iota, not a dot, will pass from the Law** until all is accomplished. Therefore **whoever relaxes one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do the same will be called least in [the kingdom of heaven](../index.md)**, but **whoever does them and teaches them will be called great in [the kingdom of heaven](../index.md)**." — Matthew 5:17-19 (ESV)

The difference is that the apostles were addressing the minimum requirements for salvation while Jesus were addressing which laws are relevant in the kingdom of heaven. As Paul also wrote in multiple letters, **salvation cannot be earned** by keeping laws, however this does not give any Christian an excuse to disobey God's laws and skip [judgement](../death/judgement.md). Obedience is a voluntary response to show love, honour and respect to the Lord Jesus Christ and to God the Father. The way that we treat the Lord Jesus Christ, God the Father and each other shows weather we have fruit of repentance or not.

This decision to reduce the burden on new converts had a very positive impact on the church's growth:

> As they went on their way through the cities, **they delivered to them for observance the decisions** that had been reached by the apostles and elders who were in Jerusalem. So the **churches were strengthened** in the faith, and they **increased in numbers daily**. — Acts 16:4-5 (ESV)
