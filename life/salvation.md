# Salvation

There are no scripture that state do this thing and you are saved forever. There are also no fixed list of steps to follow for salvation. Therefore, I have listed biblical examples that shows how people were saved. The purpose is to establish a pattern for salvation, rather than to read doctrines back into scripture.

## Salvation Before Christ

### Moses' Teaching

Moses taught Israel to love the Father and obey his commands:

> Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall **love the LORD your God** with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might. — Deuteronomy 6:4-5 (ESV)

Hebrew אָהַב (’ahav) means love, including affection. Here it calls for wholehearted inward devotion expressed in loyalty, service and obedience (Deuteronomy 11:1), not forced romantic feelings or mere outward compliance.

> And now, Israel, what does the LORD your God require of you, but
>
> - to fear the LORD your God,
> - to walk in all His ways,
> - to love Him,
> - to serve the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul, and
> - to keep the commandments and statutes of the LORD,
>
> which I am commanding you today for your good? — Deuteronomy 10:12-13

In the book of Leviticus it is also written:

> You shall therefore **keep My statutes and My rules**; if a person does them, he shall **live** by them: I am the LORD. — Leviticus 18:5 (ESV)

Moses also spoke of Israel returning after being scattered, and of the Father restoring its fortunes:

> and **return to the LORD your God**, you and your children, and **obey His voice** in all that I command you today, with all your heart and with all your soul, then the LORD your God will **restore your fortunes** and have mercy on you, and he will gather you again from all the peoples where the LORD your God has scattered you. — Deuteronomy 30:2-3 (ESV)

Isaiah calles for a restored relationship with the LORD by using intimite terms like "seek", "call". This is only possible if the wicked respond by forsaking his sinful way and to return to the the LORD:

> “**Seek the LORD** while He may be found;  
> **call upon Him** while He is near;  
> let the wicked **forsake his way**, and the unrighteous man his thoughts;
> let him **return to the LORD**, that He may have compassion on him,
> and to our God for He will abundantly **pardon**.
>
> — Isaiah 55:6-7 (ESV)

### Paul's Interpretation

Paul cites Abraham and David when discussing [faith](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith), [righteousness](righteousness.md) and [forgiveness](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/forgiveness) apart from works (Romans 4:1-8). He also connects the Father's passing over former sins with Jesus' death:

> This was to show God’s righteousness, because in His divine forbearance He had **passed over former sins**. — Romans 3:25 (ESV)

Paul's explanation does not establish that Abraham, David or other earlier believers knew the later details of Jesus' death and resurrection.

## Examples Before Christ

How did people "come to faith", without [church](https://church.ofgod.info) attendance, confession of sins, Christian baptism, or knowing Jesus Christ?

### Genesis 16:6 (Abraham)

Abraham trusted the Father, and his faith was counted as [righteousness](righteousness.md):

> And he **believed** the LORD, and He counted it to him as **righteousness**. — Genesis 15:6 (ESV)

It does not say because Abraham obeyed X, there he was saved. Instead Abraham, believed the LORD. [Believe means to trust the LORD.](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith). Trust is proven in action. Abraham had to make many significant sacrifices by trusting the LORD and that was counted as right-standing-with-God (righteousness). For example, in Genesis 15:1-6, the LORD promised Abraham an heir from his own body and offspring as numerous as the stars. Abraham believed the LORD's promise, and his belief was counted as righteousness (Romans 4:18-25; James 2:21-23).

The key is **trust**. Trust requires action. Nobody would risk action without believing that God exist or that God has their best interest in mind. This is only possible with a good standing relationship with God. Abraham's relationship with God enabled him to trust God and that was counted as righteousness. Obviously, you cannot have a good standing relationship with God if you live in sin and rebellion. That is why confession and repentance are required and not mentioned in Abraham's case because he was already in a good standing relationship with God.

### 2 Samuel 12:13 (David)

David had taken Bathsheba and arranged the death of her husband Uriah. Nathan told him a parable about a poor man's lamb. David burned with anger and judged the rich man, and Nathan answered, “You are the man” (2 Samuel 12:1-7). Nathan then announced that the sword would not depart from David's house.

> David said to Nathan, “I have **sinned** against the LORD.”  
> And Nathan said to David, “The LORD also has **put away your sin**; you shall not die.”
>
> — 2 Samuel 12:13 (ESV)

The child born of the adultery still died, and trouble stayed in David's house (2 Samuel 12:14-23). The confession brought forgiveness, but it did not remove the consequences.

Later, when David was old and could not get warm, Abishag was assigned to care for him and lie in his arms to keep him warm (1 Kings 1:1-3).

> The young woman was very beautiful, and she was of service to the king and attended to him, but the king **knew her not**. — 1 Kings 1:4 (ESV)

David did not have sexual relations with Abishag. This account does not state his motive or establish whether he committed adultery again.

Unlike the above mentioned [example of Abraham](#genesis-166-abraham), this passage contain no mention of "trust". This is because there were already multiple accounts of David trusting God. For example David had to face Goliath, King Saul, the Philistines, etc. This an example of someone who was in good standing with God, but backslide because of his sin. Therefore, you will only read about his sin and confession in this context.

The psalm describes the relief of forgiveness and the joy of being in a good standing relationship with God again. The psalm is titled “A Maskil of David.” It describes the weight of unconfessed sin and the relief that followed confession. It has long been read alongside David's sin with Bathsheba, though the psalm itself does not name the event.

> I acknowledged my sin to you, and I did not cover my iniquity; I said, “I will **confess** my transgressions to the LORD,” and you **forgave** the iniquity of my sin. — Psalm 32:5 (ESV)

The psalm then turns to instruction: the LORD promises to guide the one who trusts Him, and steadfast love surrounds him (Psalm 32:6-11). The confession is met with forgiveness, and the psalm becomes a lesson for others.

### 2 Kings 5:1-19 (Naaman)

Naaman, a Syrian commander, had leprosy. Elisha sent a messenger instructing him to wash seven times in the Jordan so that his flesh would be restored and he would be clean (2 Kings 5:1-10).

Naaman initially went away angry, expecting Elisha to come out and call on the name of the LORD. His servants persuaded him to follow the instruction. He dipped seven times in the Jordan, and his flesh was restored (2 Kings 5:11-14).

> So he went down and **dipped himself seven times in the Jordan**, according to the word of the man of God, and his flesh was **restored** like the flesh of a little child, and he was clean. — 2 Kings 5:14 (ESV)

Naaman returned to Elisha and acknowledged Israel's God. He declared that he would no longer offer sacrifices to other gods, but only to the LORD (2 Kings 5:15-17).

> “Behold, I know that there is **no God in all the earth but in Israel**” — 2 Kings 5:15 (ESV)

The commander from a pagan nation came for a physical cure and went home not only cured but convinced that Israel's God is the true God.

The washing was for physical healing. The passage does not explicitly identify it as Christian baptism or promise forgiveness of sins or eternal salvation. But it does show that Naaman shows commitment to God in his request to Elisha to pardon him when he has to serve his master who still worship Rimmon:

> Then Naaman said, “If not, please let there be given to your servant two mule loads of earth, for **from now on your servant will not offer burnt offering or sacrifice to any god but the LORD**. In this matter **may the LORD pardon your servant**: when my master goes into the house of Rimmon to worship there, leaning on my arm, and I bow myself in the house of Rimmon, **when I bow myself in the house of Rimmon, the LORD pardon your servant in this matter**.”
>
> He said to him, “Go in peace.”
>
> — 2 Kings 5:17-19 (ESV)

### Ezekiel 18:21-22 (A Wicked Person)

Ezekiel was a priest among the exiles in Babylon. The people were quoting a proverb that fathers ate sour grapes and the children's teeth were set on edge, blaming earlier generations for the exile. The Father answered through Ezekiel that each person answers for his own conduct.

> But if a wicked person **turns away from all his sins** that he has committed and **keeps all my statutes and does what is just and right**, he shall surely **live**; he shall **not die**. None of the transgressions that he has committed shall be **remembered against him**; for **the righteousness that he has done he shall live**. — Ezekiel 18:21-22 (ESV)

The people replied that the way of the LORD was not just (Ezekiel 18:25,29). Then the chapter ends with the same appeal:

> **Repent** and turn from all your transgressions… For I have no pleasure in the death of anyone… so turn, and live — Ezekiel 18:30-32

Each hearer was called to turn and live.

## Examples by Christ

The sections below cover the passages in both [tables](#salvation) in their contexts.

### Matthew 3:5-12; John 3:33-36 (Judea)

After about many centuries without a prophet, John appeared in the Judean wilderness by the Jordan. His clothing and diet marked him as a prophet, and he came to prepare Israel for the Lord, as Isaiah had foretold.

> Then Jerusalem and all Judea and all the region about the Jordan were going out to him *[John the Baptist]*, and they were **baptized by him in the river Jordan, confessing their sins**.
>
> But when he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to his baptism, he said to them, “You brood of vipers! Who warned you to flee from **the wrath to come**? **Bear fruit in keeping with repentance.** And do not presume to say to yourselves, ‘We have Abraham as our father,’ for I tell you, God is able from these stones to raise up children for Abraham. Even now **the axe is laid to the root of the trees. Every tree therefore that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire.**
>
> I **baptize you with water for repentance**, but he who is coming after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire. His **winnowing fork** is in his hand, and he will clear his threshing floor and **gather his wheat into the barn, but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire**.”
>
> — Matthew 3:5-12

The crowds present were from three regions and publicly admitted their sins, the movement reached the religious leaders, and Jesus himself came to be baptized (3:13-17). John message is clear: He warns the people about coming judgement and urge his followers to repent so that they could bear "good fruit" to avoid being "cut down and thrown into fire".

In John the Baptist's last recorded testimony: His disciples were arguing about purification with a Jew and were jealous that crowds were leaving John for Jesus, who was baptizing in the Judean countryside (John 3:22-26). John answered that a man can receive only what is given from God ([heaven](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/heaven)), that he is the friend of the bridegroom, and that Jesus must increase (John 3:27-30). Then John wrote:

> Whoever receives **his testimony sets his seal to this, that God is true**. For [he whom God has sent utters the words of God](https://son.ofgod.info/shema/agency), for He *[God]* gives *the Spirit* without measure. The Father loves the Son and has given all things into his hand. **Whoever believes** in the Son **has eternal life**; **whoever does not obey** the Son shall not see life, but the **wrath of God** remains on him. — John 3:33-36 (ESV)

John explains [biblical agency](https://son.ofgod.info/shema/agency). God is the source of all things. His agent "utters the words of God".

What is this important testimony?

> that God is true.

> [!NOTE]
> There is a textual variant regarding "he gives the Spirit without measure."
>
> "he" is read in some manuscripts as “God.” A(02) D(05) Latin(a d) BYZ TR.
> "the Spirit" is absent from the B(03) manuscript. John more likely meant that God gives all words through the Son, rather than giving the Spirit which is a different matter from this context. Either interpretation could be correct as God also provides his words through His Spirit.

Then John addresses the reason why we should belief the Son's testimony, which is that "the Father loves the Son" and "the Father has given all things into his hand" shows that the Father trusts him, therefore we too should trust him.

"Believe" does not mean to blindly accept a paradox. Since the testimony is about a distinct God that sent His messenger, it is very unlikely that John intended his readers to understand that they needed to belief "in" God's messenger (the Son) as their supreme God.

["Believe" means to trust.](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith) In this case trust the Son's testimony and his commands that we should obey.

John's last recorded word placed life and wrath on how a hearer responded to the Son, not on ancestry or ritual.

### Matthew 4-7 (Galilee)

After Jesus' baptism and temptation, he heard that John had been arrested. He withdrew to Galilee and made Capernaum his base, fulfilling Isaiah's word about light dawning on Zebulun and Naphtali (Matthew 4:12-16).

> “**Repent**, for the **kingdom of heaven is at hand**.” — Matthew 4:17 (ESV)

> “The time is fulfilled, and the **kingdom of God is at hand**; **repent** and **believe in the gospel**.” — Mark 1:14-15 (ESV)

Mark adds that Jesus came into Galilee “proclaiming the gospel of God.” He at once called Peter, Andrew, James and John from their nets (Matthew 4:18-22), taught in the synagogues, healed the sick, and great crowds followed from Galilee, Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea and beyond the Jordan (Matthew 4:23-25). John's call continued, but now the kingdom was announced by the one who would rule it, and the call moved from the desert to the towns.

Matthew also provide details about a particular sermon on the mount in Galilee which include teaching about prayer, money and judging others, etc. I cannot quote all details here, but I would like to highlight the closing of this sermon:

> “So whatever you wish that others would do to you, **do also** to them, for this is **the Law** and the Prophets.
>
> Enter by the narrow gate.
>
> For the gate is wide and the way is easy that leads to destruction, and those who enter by it are many.
> For the gate is narrow and the way is hard that leads to life, and those who find it are few.
>
> Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep’s clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves. You will **recognize them by their fruits**. Are grapes gathered from thornbushes, or figs from thistles? So, every healthy tree bears good fruit, but the diseased tree bears bad fruit. A healthy tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a diseased tree bear good fruit. Every tree that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire. Thus you will **recognize them by their fruits**.
>
> Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, **but the one who does the will of my Father** who is in heaven.
>
> **On that day** many will say to me, ‘Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and do many mighty works in your name?’  
> And then will I declare to them, ‘I never knew you; depart from me, you workers of lawlessness.’
>
> Everyone then who hears these words of mine and **does them will be like a wise man** who built his house on the rock. And the rain fell, and the floods came, and the winds blew and beat on that house, but it did not fall, because it had been founded on the rock. And everyone who hears these words of mine and **does not do them will be like a foolish man** who built his house on the sand. And the rain fell, and the floods came, and the winds blew and beat against that house, and it fell, and great was the fall of it.”
>
> And when Jesus finished these sayings, the crowds were astonished at his teaching, for he was teaching them as **one who had authority**, and not as their scribes.
>
> — Matthew 7:12-29 (ESV)

The sermon ends with the crowds astonished at his authority, unlike the scribes (7:28-29). The sermon closes not with comfort but with a test. Hearing was not enough. The hearer had to obey. The judgement named here is the destruction at the wide gate and the “I never knew you” of Matthew 7:23.

### Luke 19:1-10 (Zacchaeus)

Jesus entered Jericho and was passing through. Zacchaeus, a chief tax collector and a rich man, wanted to see who Jesus was. Because he was short and the crowd blocked his view, he ran ahead and climbed a sycamore tree. When Jesus reached the place, he looked up, called Zacchaeus by name, and told him to come down quickly because he must stay at his house that day. Zacchaeus hurried down and welcomed Jesus joyfully.

> And when they saw it, they all grumbled, “He has gone in to be the guest of **a man who is a sinner**.”
>
> And Zacchaeus stood and said to the Lord, “Behold, Lord, the **half of my goods I give** to the poor. And if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold.”  
> And Jesus said to him, “Today **salvation** has come to this house”, since he also is a son of Abraham. For the Son of Man came to **seek and to save** the lost.
>
> — Luke 19:7-10 (ESV)

His response turned away from taking from people and toward giving and making restitution. Luke does not record a separate spoken command to repent, but instead, he shows Zacchaeus's conversion through his joyful welcome of Jesus and the concrete changes he declared. Therefore was can assume Zacchaeus repented. Jesus then announced that salvation had come to Zacchaeus's house.

### John 4:39-42 (Samaritans)

Jesus was traveling from Judea to Galilee and chose the road through Samaria. At Jacob's well near Sychar he sat alone while the disciples went to buy food. A Samaritan woman came at noon, and he asked her for a drink. Jesus exposed her five marriages, taught that worship would not be tied to a mountain, and said, “I who speak to you am he” (John 4:26). She told the town, and many Samaritans responded:

> Many Samaritans from that town **believed in him** because of the woman’s testimony, “He told me all that I ever did.”
>
> So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them, and he stayed there two days. And **many more believed because of his word**.
>
> They said to the woman, “It is no longer because of what you said that **we believe**, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is indeed *[truly the Christ]* the Savior of the world.”
>
> — John 4:39-42 (ESV)

> [!NOTE]
> The manuscripts of A(02) D(05) Latin(d e) BYZ TR includes "truly the Christ" while other omits it. Compare for example with KJV. Either way it implies that the Samaritans trusted that Jesus would save the world as the Christ (John 4:25-26).

There is not much recorded about the Jesus ministry in Samaria besides that he stayed with them for 2 days.

### John 5:21-26 (Jewish Leaders)

Jesus was in Jerusalem for a feast. He had healed a man lame for thirty-eight years at the pool of Bethesda, and he did it on the Sabbath. The Jewish leaders began to persecute him, and he answered that his Father was still working (John 5:1-18).

> So Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, [the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the Father does, that the Son does likewise.](https://son.ofgod.info/shema/agency) For the Father loves the Son and shows him all that he himself is doing. And greater works than these will he show him, so that you may marvel. For as **the Father raises the dead and gives them life**, so also the Son gives life to whom he will. For **the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father**. **Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father Who sent him.**
>
> Truly, truly, I say to you, **whoever hears my word and believes Him Who sent me has eternal life**. He does not come into **judgment**, but has passed from death to life.
>
> Truly, truly, I say to you, an hour is coming, and is now here, when **the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live**. For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself. And he has given him authority to execute **judgment**, because he is the Son of Man. Do not marvel at this, for an hour is coming when all who are in **the tombs will hear his voice and come out**, those who have done good to **the resurrection of life**, and those who have done evil to **the resurrection of judgment**.
>
> I can do nothing on my own. As I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just, because [I seek not my own will but the will of Him who sent me](https://son.ofgod.info/shema/agency).
>
> — John 5:19-30 (ESV)

Jesus went on to name John the Baptist, his own works, the Father and the Scriptures as witnesses, and said Moses would accuse them (John 5:30-47). A Sabbath healing became a claim to give life, and the leaders' opposition hardened. No explicit call is recorded because the passage describes Jesus explanation of God's delegated authority rather than issuing a command.

### John 8:12-30 (Jews)

It was the Feast of Booths in Jerusalem, the Pharisees were offended by Jesus teaching claiming that he is deceiving the crowds. The officers refused to capture Jesus and Nicodemus defended Jesus by saying the Pharisees should first judge Jesus (John 7:40-52).

> [!WARNING]
> John 7:53-8:11 known as the "Pericope Adulterae Interpolation".
>
>> It is absent from the earliest manuscripts including 𝔓66, 𝔓75, ℵ(01), B(03), W(032) Latin(a) and Syriac(sy). Absent from THGNT. Double bracketed in NA28 and SBLGNT. Included in D(05) Latin(d e ff2) BYZ and TR. UBS Handbooks for New Testament (20 vol.) states regarding the pericope, “is not found in the earlier and better Greek manuscripts, it differs in style and vocabulary from the rest of John's Gospel, and it interrupts the sequence of 7.52 and 8.12 and following.” — [Footnote on AICNT](https://corebible.app/JHN/8)

They meet Jesus at the treasury and most likely questioned whether Jesus was speaking the truth on which Jesus replied:

> Again Jesus spoke to them, saying, “I am [the light](../light.md) of the world. Whoever follows me will not walk in [darkness](../darkness.md), but will have the light of life.” — John 8:12 (ESV)

The Pharisees challenged his witness in response on which Jesus defended that his Father Himself is his witness (John 8:13-15). Then he warns them that he is the Father's [delegated judge](https://son.ofgod.info/shema/agency):

> ... for it is **not I alone who judge, but I and the Father [Who sent me](https://son.ofgod.info/shema/agency)**. In your Law it is written that **the testimony of two people is true**.” — John 8:16-17 (ESV)

The Pharisees replied that Jesus's witness is invalid because they do not know his Father on which Jesus replied that he represents the Father. (John 8:18-20)

> So he said to them again, “I am going away, and you will seek me, and **you will die in your sin**. Where I am going, you cannot come.”  
> So the Jews said, “Will he kill himself, since he says, ‘Where I am going, you cannot come’?”

## Examples after Christ

### Mark 16:14-16 (Disciples)

After the resurrection, Jesus appeared to the eleven disciples while they were at table. They had not believed the earlier reports from Mary Magdalene and from the two travelers to Emmaus, so he rebuked their unbelief and hardness of heart.

> Afterward he *[Jesus]* appeared to the eleven themselves as they were reclining at table, and he rebuked them for their unbelief and hardness of heart, because they had **not believed those who saw him after he had risen**. And he said to them, “Go into all the world and proclaim the gospel to the whole creation. **Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved**, but **whoever does not [believe](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/faith) will be condemned**.” — Mark 16:14-16 (ESV)

The Gospel ends with the Lord working with them as they preached everywhere (Mark 16:20). The call widened from Israel to the whole creation, and baptism was named as part of the response.

### Acts 2:22-36 (Peter)

Peter addresses Israel, identifies Jesus as a man attested by God, says God raised him, and announces that God made him both Lord and Christ. Asked what to do, he calls for repentance and baptism in Jesus Christ’s name (Acts 2:14–41). The account continues through the community’s life and its stated salvation outcome (Acts 2:42–47):

> Men of Israel, hear these words:
>
> Jesus of Nazareth, [**a man** attested to you by God with mighty works and wonders and signs that God did through him in your midst](https://son.ofgod.info/shema/agency), as you yourselves know — this Jesus, delivered up according to **[the definite plan and foreknowledge of God](https://son.ofgod.info/name/word)**, you [crucified and killed](../christ/crucifixion.md) by the hands of **lawless** men. [God raised him up](../death/resurrection.md), loosing the pangs of death, because it was not possible for him to be held by it. For David says concerning him,
>
>> “‘I saw the Lord always before me, for he is at my right hand that I may not be shaken;  
>> therefore my heart was glad, and my tongue rejoiced;

>>

>> You have made known to me the paths of life;  
>> You will make me full of gladness with your presence.’
>>
>> — Psalm 16:8–11
>
> “Brothers, I may say to you with confidence about the patriarch David that he both died and was buried, and his tomb is with us to this day. Being therefore a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him that he would set **one of his descendants on his throne**, [he foresaw](https://prophecies.ofgod.info/messianic) and spoke about **the resurrection of the Christ**, that he was not abandoned to *Hades*, nor did his flesh see corruption. **[This Jesus God raised up](../death/resurrection.md)**, and of that we all are witnesses. Being therefore **exalted at the right hand of God**, and having received from the Father the promise of the Holy Spirit, he has poured out this that you yourselves are seeing and hearing. For David did not ascend into the heavens, but he himself says,

>
> Now when they heard this they were cut to the heart, and said to Peter and the rest of the apostles, “Brothers, what shall we do?”
> And Peter said to them, “**Repent** and **be baptized** every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ **for the forgiveness of your sins**, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. For the promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off, everyone whom the Lord our God calls to himself.”

> And with many other words he bore witness and continued to exhort them, saying, “Save yourselves from this crooked generation.” So those who received his word were **baptized**, and there were added that day about **three thousand souls**. And **awe came upon every soul**, and many wonders and signs were being done through the apostles.

In this sermon, Peter:

- Identified [Jesus as a human](https://son.ofgod.info/human)
- Identified [Jesus as God's delegated agent](https://son.ofgod.info/shema/agency)
- Shows that [Jesus was part of God's definite plan and foreknowledge](https://son.ofgod.info/name/word)
- Testified that Jesus was risen by God by quoting Psalm 16:8-11
- Points out that Jesus was a descendend of David, fulfilling [prophecies](https://prophecies.ofgod.info/messianic)
- God exalted Jesus to His right hand as a reference to his received authority
- Jesus is the [Christ](../christ.md) meaning "anointed by God" to be our lord
- He also call to repentance and baptism for the forgiveness of sin

> [!NOTE]
> But Peter did **not** call for a confession that *Jesus is the Almighty God* or a *[three-person, one-essence doctrine](https://son.ofgod.info/trinity)*. Yet about **3000** souls were added that day (Acts 2:41) despite hearing nothing about [the divinity of Jesus](https://son.ofgod.info/divine). The Lord even continues to add more souls with this message (Acts 2:47).
