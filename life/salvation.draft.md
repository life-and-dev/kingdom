# Life

There are many false teaching about "Salvation" in circulation. Instead of blindly following a doctrine, let's look at what the Bible actually says.

> - `-` means no evidence recorded, not proof that a teaching or event never occurred.
> - `Assume` means a response reasonably inferred from audience reaction, not directly recorded.

## Context Details

The sections below cover the passages in both [tables](#summaries). Where a response continues the same scene, the request and the response share one sub-section.

### John 6:35 — The Bread of Life

The day after Jesus fed five thousand near the Sea of Galilee, the crowd followed him to Capernaum. They had eaten the bread and wanted more. Jesus told them not to work for food that perishes but for the food that endures to eternal life (6:22-27).

> “I am the **bread of life**; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever **believes in me** shall never thirst.” — John 6:35 (ESV)

They asked for a sign like the manna, and Jesus answered that the Father gives the true bread from heaven (6:30-33). The Jews then grumbled at his claim to have come down from heaven, and many disciples turned back. Peter answered, “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life” (6:60-68). The difference: the offer was personal and repeatable, coming and believing, not eating literal bread.

### John 8:12-30 — The Light of the World

It was the Feast of Booths in Jerusalem, and Jesus was teaching in the treasury, near the great lamps lit for the feast. The Pharisees had just tested him with a woman caught in adultery (7:53-8:11).

> “I am the **light of the world**. Whoever **follows me** will not walk in darkness, but will have the **light of life**.” — John 8:12 (ESV)

> “As he was saying these things, **many believed in him**.” — John 8:30 (ESV)

The Pharisees objected that his testimony was not valid because he testified about himself (8:13). Jesus spoke of his Father and warned that they would die in their sins unless they believed. The Response table records that many believed at this point. He told those believers that abiding in his word would make them free (8:31-32), and the exchange ended with an attempt to stone him (8:59). The difference: light and life were offered in the middle of a public dispute. Some believed, but the leaders grew more hostile.

### John 10:10 — Life Abundantly

Jesus had healed a man born blind, and the Pharisees had thrown him out of the synagogue (9:34). Jesus found him and spoke about shepherds, sheep and thieves, with the Pharisees listening.

> “The thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy. I came that they may have **life** and have it **abundantly**.” — John 10:10 (ESV)

He had just called himself the door of the sheep (10:9). He then called himself the good shepherd who lays down his life for the sheep (10:11-18), and the crowd divided again (10:19-21). The difference: life is set against the damage done by false shepherds, and the shepherd's own death is named as the means.

### John 11:25-26 — The Resurrection and the Life

Lazarus of Bethany, brother of Mary and Martha, was sick. Jesus delayed two days and arrived after Lazarus had been in the tomb four days. Martha went out to meet him on the road.

> “I am the **resurrection and the life**. Whoever **believes in me**, though he die, yet shall he live, and everyone who lives and believes in me shall never die. Do you believe this?” — John 11:25-26 (ESV)

Martha had said, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died,” and Jesus answered that her brother would rise again (11:21-23). She confessed, “Yes, Lord; I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God” (11:27). Jesus wept, raised Lazarus, and many Jews believed (11:45). The Sanhedrin then plotted to kill Jesus (11:47-53). The difference: the promise was spoken to one grieving sister and then demonstrated at the tomb.

### John 12:44-50 — The Word That Judges

This is the end of Jesus' public ministry, after the triumphal entry and after Greeks asked to see him. John notes that many believed but would not confess him for fear of the Pharisees (12:42-43).

> “I have come into the world as light, so that whoever **believes in me** may not remain in darkness. If anyone hears my words and **does not keep them**, I do not judge him; for I did not come to judge the world but to **save the world**. The one who rejects me and does not receive my words has a judge; the word that I have spoken will **judge him on the last day**.” — John 12:46-48 (ESV)

Jesus cried out that whoever believes in him believes in the one who sent him (12:44). He said he spoke what the Father commanded. John had already noted that Jesus hid himself from them (12:36), and this cry closes his public teaching (12:49-50). The difference: judgement is deferred to the last day and tied to the words already spoken, while the immediate mission was to save.

### John 14:6 — The Way, the Truth, and the Life

It was the night before the crucifixion. Jesus had washed the disciples' feet, Judas had left, and Jesus had told Peter that he would deny him. He began, “Let not your hearts be troubled” (14:1).

> “I am **the way**, and **the truth**, and **the life**. No one comes to the Father except through me.” — John 14:6 (ESV)

Thomas said, “Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?” (14:5). Philip asked to see the Father, and Jesus pointed him back to his words and works (14:8-11), promised the Spirit, and said, “Rise, let us go from here” (14:31). The difference: on the night their leader was taken, the disciples were given a person to follow, not a map.

### Acts 2:5-47 — Pentecost

Fifty days after Passover, Jews and proselytes from many nations were in Jerusalem for the Feast of Weeks. The Spirit came on the disciples, they spoke in other languages, and some in the crowd mocked them as drunk (2:1-13).

> “And it shall come to pass that everyone who **calls upon the name of the Lord** shall be **saved**.” … “Let all the house of Israel therefore know for certain that God has **made him both Lord and Christ**, this Jesus whom you crucified.” … “**Repent** and be **baptized** every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the **forgiveness of your sins**, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.” — Acts 2:21, 36, 38 (ESV)

> “So those who received his word were **baptized**, and there were added that day about three thousand souls. And they **devoted themselves** to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers.” — Acts 2:41-42 (ESV)

Peter explained the tongues as Joel's promised outpouring and quoted Joel's warning about the day of the Lord (2:17-21), which is why `Judgement` is marked `Yes`. He also used David's psalm for the resurrection (2:25-31), which is why `Plan` is marked `Yes`. The hearers were “cut to the heart” and asked what to do (2:37). The Response table continues the same scene: three thousand were baptized and added that day, and they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching, fellowship, bread and prayers. They shared their possessions and praised God with glad hearts (2:43-47). The difference: this was the first public call after the resurrection, and the first large response.

### Acts 3:11-26; 4:4 — Times of Refreshing

Peter and John were going to the temple at the ninth hour, the hour of prayer. A man lame from birth asked them for money at the Beautiful Gate. Peter healed him in Jesus' name, the man leaped and praised God, and a crowd gathered at Solomon's portico.

> “**Repent** therefore, and **turn back**, that your **sins may be blotted out**, that times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord, and that he may send the Christ appointed for you, Jesus.” … “Moses said, ‘The Lord God will **raise up for you a prophet** like me from your brothers. **You shall listen to him** in whatever he tells you.’ … And it shall be that every soul who does not listen to that prophet shall be **destroyed from the people**.” — Acts 3:19-23 (ESV)

> “But many of those who had heard the word **believed**, and the number of the men came to about **five thousand**.” — Acts 4:4 (ESV)

Peter told the amazed crowd that the healing was not by their own power. He said they had denied the Holy and Righteous One and killed the Author of life, whom God raised (3:12-16). The Response table records the outcome of the same sermon: many believed, and the number of men came to about five thousand. The priests, the captain of the temple and the Sadducees then arrested them (4:1-4). The difference: a healing at the temple gate became a public call to turn, and the opposition began.

### Acts 4:8-22 — No Other Name

The next day the rulers, elders and scribes met in Jerusalem with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas, John and Alexander. They set Peter and John in the midst and asked, “By what power or by what name did you do this?”

> “This Jesus is the **stone that was rejected** by you, the builders, which has become the cornerstone. And there is **salvation** in no one else, for there is **no other name** under heaven given among men by which we must be **saved**.” — Acts 4:11-12 (ESV)

Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said the man stood well by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom they crucified and God raised (4:8-10). `Plan` is marked `Yes` because Peter cites Psalm 118:22, the rejected stone that became the cornerstone. The rulers saw the boldness of uneducated men and the healed man standing with them, and could say nothing against it. They warned them not to speak in the name and released them (4:13-22). The believers then prayed, the place shook, and they spoke with boldness (4:23-31). The difference: the name they were ordered to suppress became the center of the church's public prayer.

### Acts 5:29-42 — Obey God Rather Than Men

The apostles had been arrested, freed by an angel, and were teaching in the temple again. The council and the high priest brought them in and reminded them of the order not to teach in this name.

> “We must obey God rather than men. The God of our fathers raised Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree. God exalted him at his right hand as Leader and Savior, to give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins.” — Acts 5:29-31 (ESV)

The high priest accused them of filling Jerusalem with their teaching and of intending to bring Jesus' blood on them (5:28). The council was furious and wanted to kill them, but Gamaliel advised caution. They were beaten, charged again and released. The apostles left “rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the name” and kept teaching daily (5:40-42). No call is recorded here: this is a defense before hostile rulers. The difference: the apostles counted suffering for the name as honor instead of backing down.

### Acts 8:5-13 — Good News in Samaria

Persecution after Stephen's death scattered the Jerusalem church. Philip, one of the seven chosen to serve tables, went down to a city of Samaria, where a magician named Simon had amazed the people for a long time.

> “Philip went down to the city of Samaria and **proclaimed to them the Christ**.” … “But when they **believed** Philip as he preached good news about the **kingdom of God** and the name of Jesus Christ, they were **baptized**, both men and women.” — Acts 8:5, 12 (ESV)

> “Even Simon himself **believed**, and after being **baptized** he continued with Philip.” — Acts 8:13 (ESV)

The crowds paid attention to Philip because of the signs he did: unclean spirits came out, and the paralyzed and lame were healed (8:6-7). The Response table records that even Simon believed and stayed with Philip, amazed at the signs. The apostles sent Peter and John, who prayed and laid hands on the Samaritans so they received the Spirit (8:14-17). Simon then tried to buy the power and was rebuked (8:18-24). The difference: the gospel crossed from Jews to Samaritans, a group with centuries of hostility.

### Acts 8:26-40 — The Ethiopian Reads Isaiah

An angel told Philip to go south on the desert road from Jerusalem to Gaza. There he met an Ethiopian eunuch, a court official in charge of the treasury of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians. The man had been to Jerusalem to worship and was reading Isaiah aloud.

> “Then Philip opened his mouth, and **beginning with this Scripture** he told him the good news about Jesus.” … “And he commanded the chariot to stop, and they both went down into the water, Philip and the eunuch, and he baptized him.” — Acts 8:35, 38 (ESV)

> “And the eunuch saw him no more, and **went on his way rejoicing**.” — Acts 8:39 (ESV)

The eunuch was reading Isaiah 53:7-8 and asked whether the prophet spoke of himself or someone else (8:30-34). After the baptism, the Spirit carried Philip away (8:39). Philip preached in Azotus and the towns up to Caesarea (8:40). The difference: the plan announced in Isaiah was applied to one foreign official, which is why `Plan` is marked `Yes`.

### Acts 9:1-19, 22:12-16 — Saul Baptized

Saul was a young Pharisee who had approved Stephen's death and was “breathing threats and murder” against the disciples. He got letters from the high priest to the synagogues in Damascus so he could arrest believers there. On the road a light from heaven flashed, and he fell to the ground.

> “Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?” … “I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.” — Acts 9:4-5 (ESV)

> “And now why do you wait? Rise and be **baptized** and **wash away your sins**, **calling on his name**.” — Acts 22:16 (ESV)

> “Then he rose and was **baptized**; and taking food, he was strengthened.” — Acts 9:18-19 (ESV)

Saul was blind for three days and did not eat or drink (9:9). Ananias was sent by a vision and called him “Brother Saul” (9:10-17). Something like scales fell from his eyes (9:18). He immediately proclaimed Jesus in the Damascus synagogues, and the Jews plotted to kill him; he escaped in a basket (9:20-25). The difference: the chief persecutor became the chief witness.

### Acts 10:34-48 — No Partiality

Cornelius was a centurion of the Italian cohort in Caesarea, a devout man who gave alms and prayed constantly. An angel told him to send for Peter, who was staying in Joppa and had just received a vision of a sheet with unclean animals. Peter came to Cornelius' house, where relatives and close friends were gathered.

> “Truly I understand that **God shows no partiality**, but in every nation anyone who **fears him** and does what is right is acceptable to him.” … “And he commanded us to preach to the people and to testify that he is the one appointed by God to be **judge of the living and the dead**. To him all the prophets bear witness that everyone who **believes in him** receives **forgiveness of sins** through his name.” — Acts 10:34-35, 42-43 (ESV)

> “While Peter was still saying these things, the **Holy Spirit fell** on all who heard the word… For they were hearing them speaking in **tongues** and **extolling God**. … And he commanded them to be **baptized** in the name of Jesus Christ.” — Acts 10:44-48 (ESV)

Peter said, “You yourselves know that it is unlawful for a Jew to associate with or to visit a Gentile, but God has shown me that I should not call any person common or unclean” (10:28). The Response table records what happened while he was still speaking: the Spirit fell, the circumcised believers were amazed, and the household was baptized. `Plan` is marked `Yes` because Peter says all the prophets bear witness to him (10:43), and `Judgement` because he names Jesus as judge of the living and the dead. The difference: this was the first Gentile household to receive the Spirit.

### Acts 13:16-52 — Forgiveness Proclaimed

Paul and Barnabas were on their first journey and came to Pisidian Antioch. On the Sabbath they went into the synagogue, and after the reading of the Law and the Prophets the rulers invited them to speak.

> “Let it be known to you therefore, brothers, that through this man **forgiveness of sins** is proclaimed to you, and by him everyone who **believes** is **freed** from everything from which you could not be freed by the law of Moses.” — Acts 13:38-39 (ESV)

> “And when the Gentiles heard this, they began **rejoicing** and **glorifying the word of the Lord**… And the disciples were filled with **joy** and with the **Holy Spirit**.” — Acts 13:48, 52 (ESV)

Paul reviewed Israel's history from Egypt to David and announced that God had brought to Israel a Savior, Jesus, as promised (13:17-23), which is why `Plan` is marked `Yes`. He said the rulers in Jerusalem had fulfilled the prophets by condemning him (13:27-29). The Gentiles begged to hear more the next Sabbath, and almost the whole city gathered. The Jews were filled with jealousy and contradicted Paul, so Paul and Barnabas turned to the Gentiles. The Response table records that the Gentiles rejoiced, glorified the word, and followed Paul; the Jews stirred up persecution and drove them out. They shook off the dust and went to Iconium (13:42-52). The difference: the message moved from the synagogue to the Gentiles, and the city heard it.

### Acts 16:11-15 — Lydia's Heart Opened

Paul had a vision of a man from Macedonia asking for help, so the team sailed from Troas to Philippi, a Roman colony. On the Sabbath they looked for a place of prayer and found women gathered by the river outside the gate.

> “The Lord **opened her heart** to pay attention to what was said by Paul. And after she was **baptized**, and her household as well, she urged us, saying, ‘If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come to my house and stay.’ And she prevailed upon us.” — Acts 16:14-15 (ESV)

Lydia was from Thyatira, a seller of purple goods, and a worshiper of God (16:14). Her house became the base for the mission. Later Paul and Silas were beaten and jailed, and after the earthquake they returned to Lydia's house, encouraged the brothers and departed (16:40). The difference: the first believer recorded in Philippi, and no call is recorded because the emphasis is on the Lord opening her heart.

### Acts 16:25-34 — What Must I Do to Be Saved?

Paul and Silas had been beaten with rods and put in the inner prison with their feet in stocks. About midnight they were praying and singing hymns, and the other prisoners were listening. An earthquake opened the doors and loosened the chains.

> “Sirs, **what must I do to be saved**?” And they said, “**Believe in the Lord Jesus**, and you will be **saved**, you and your household.” … and he was **baptized** at once, he and all his family. — Acts 16:30-31, 33 (ESV)

> “And he **rejoiced** along with his entire household that he had believed in God.” — Acts 16:34 (ESV)

The jailer woke, saw the doors open and drew his sword to kill himself, thinking the prisoners had escaped. Paul called out that they were all there (16:27-28). The jailer washed their wounds and set food before them (16:33-34). The next day the magistrates released them (16:35-40). The difference: the question came from a frightened Roman jailer, and the answer was a person to trust, followed by baptism. `Baptize` stays `-` because the stated condition was belief, not the baptism that followed.

### Acts 17:16-34 — The Unknown God

Paul was waiting for Silas and Timothy in Athens. He was provoked by the city's idols, so he reasoned in the synagogue, in the marketplace, and with Epicurean and Stoic philosophers, who brought him to the Areopagus.

> “The times of ignorance God overlooked, but now he **commands all people everywhere to repent**, because he has fixed a day on which he will **judge the world** in righteousness by a man whom he has appointed; and of this he has given assurance to all by **raising him from the dead**.” — Acts 17:30-31 (ESV)

> “But some men **joined him** and **believed**, among whom also were Dionysius the Areopagite and a woman named Damaris and others with them.” — Acts 17:34 (ESV)

Paul began with their altar “to the unknown god” and quoted their own poets, saying God made the world and does not live in temples made by hands (17:22-29). Some mocked the resurrection, and others said, “We will hear you again about this” (17:32). `Repent` and `Judgement` are marked `Yes`, but `Risen` is `-`: the resurrection is named as proof of the coming judge, not as the object of a call to trust. The difference: a debate about an unknown god ended with a command to repent and a warning of judgement; some mocked, but a few joined Paul.

### Acts 26:1-32 — Turning from Darkness

Paul had been held in Caesarea for two years. He was brought before King Agrippa and Bernice, with Festus the governor present, to explain the charges against him. He told the story of the Damascus road and repeated the commission Jesus gave him.

> “To open their eyes, so that they may **turn from darkness to light** and from the power of Satan to God, that they may receive **forgiveness of sins** and a place among those who are sanctified by **faith in me**.” — Acts 26:18 (ESV)

Jesus told him he was appointing him as a servant and witness to Jews and Gentiles (26:16-17). He said he had preached repentance in Damascus, Jerusalem, Judea and to the Gentiles, and that this was why the Jews seized him (26:20-21). He also said he taught nothing beyond what Moses and the prophets said would happen, that the Christ must suffer and rise (26:22-23), which is why `Plan` is marked `Yes`. Festus shouted, “Paul, you are out of your mind.” Agrippa said, “In a short time would you persuade me to be a Christian?” The officials agreed he had done nothing deserving death, but he had appealed to Caesar (26:24-32). The difference: the commission itself defined the change as turning from darkness to light.

### Acts 26:20 — Deeds in Keeping with Repentance

In the same defense, Paul summarized what he had preached everywhere before his arrest.

> “but declared first to those in Damascus, then in Jerusalem and throughout all the region of Judea, and also to the Gentiles, that they should **repent** and **turn to God**, **performing deeds in keeping with their repentance**.” — Acts 26:20 (ESV)

He said, “I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision” (26:19). He then explained that this preaching was the reason the Jews seized him in the temple (26:21). Only `Repent` is marked `Yes`. The difference: the call was not a one-time prayer but a turn of life with deeds that match it.

### 1 John 5:12 — Life in the Son

John wrote near the end of his first letter to congregations troubled by teachers who had left and denied that Jesus came in the flesh (2:18-19; 4:1-3). He had just said that God gave us eternal life and that this life is in his Son.

> “Whoever **has the Son** has **life**; whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life.” — 1 John 5:12 (ESV)

It is preceded by “And this is the testimony, that God gave us eternal life, and this life is in his Son” (5:11). It is followed by “I write these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, that you may know that you have eternal life” (5:13), then by confidence in prayer and a closing warning to keep away from idols (5:14-21). The difference: the letter gives assurance to ordinary believers. No call and no response are recorded because the verse states a fact rather than issuing an invitation.
